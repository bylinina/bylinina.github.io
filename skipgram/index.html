Sentences

Each sentence is lowercased, split into words, and stripped of stop words.

Training pairs

Every word is paired with each word up to 2 positions away in its sentence. Each cell counts how often a pair occurs: that is how many real examples it gives per pass over the corpus. Every real example also gets k = 2 random examples: the same word with random words, drawn in proportion to frequency0.75.

Rows = word; columns = word in its window.

Training

Every word starts with two random vectors of 5 numbers: one for when it is the word in the middle (w), one for when it is in someone else's window (c). The probability that a pair is real is σ(c · w). Each step pulls a real pair together and pushes its random pairs apart.

The map squeezes the 5 dimensions of each word's w vector into two, just for display. Similarities are computed in the full space.

Similar words

After training, words are compared by the angle between their w vectors (cosine similarity: 1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated).

Similar, but never in each other's window

Pairs of words that never occur within 2 positions of each other but share at least 3 window words. Their vectors end up close because the same context vectors pull on both.
